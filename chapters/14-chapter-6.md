The pipes ran in every direction, lining the walls and crossing the ceiling and diving into the floor at angles that suggested the people who built this place had not been thinking about the people who would have to walk through it. Three levels below the service access, where Maeve had stayed behind to stand guard, the air tasted of metal and something chemical. Condensation beaded on the surfaces around them, catching the thin emergency lighting in small bright points that could have been eyes.

Eli counted seven people ahead of him in the passage, counting Luca. Maeve handpicked these soldiers, he could tell, by the way they always carried their weapons with the intention to draw them. Behind him, two more. He could hear them without turning. The sound their boots made on the grated floor was different, more painful to listen to than on the cobblestones in Auralis.

The walls were grey steel, bolted at even intervals, each joint sealed with a precision that left no room for anything to grow. No cracks where moss might find purchase. No imperfection of any kind. The corridor ran ahead in a line so straight it seemed to narrow at the far end, collapsing into a vanishing point the emergency lights could not reach.

Luca walked at the front. He moved through the dark the way a person moves through their own home, his steps never hesitating, his hand never reaching for the wall. He did not look back. He had not looked back since they crossed in, and in the twenty minutes since, the distance between Luca and the rest of the team had stayed a gap of about four paces that nobody had been instructed to maintain and nobody was willing to close.

Eli walked in the middle of the gap. He could feel it on both sides of him. Ahead, Luca's silence. Behind, the particular quality of attention that soldiers direct at a person they have been told not to kill.

The passage narrowed. Eli kept his wings pulled tight and still they caught on things. Every small protrusion this corridor had to offer found them, dragging through the feathers, snagging, slowing him by a fraction each time. He stopped walking. He closed his eyes. When he opened them the wings were gone. He rolled his shoulders once and the absence settled into place. If something came around the next corner he would have to meet it. Then again, he thought, looking at the ceiling barely a hand's width above his head, the walls close enough to touch with both elbows bent, there was nowhere here to fly to.

They had been descending for a while. The angle was gentle enough that it registered in the knees before the mind, a slow accumulation of downward that Eli tracked by the pressure in his ears and the change in the air. It was warmer here. Thicker. He thought of Auralis. He thought of the market in the early morning, the air coming through from the residential quarter carrying bread and lavender.

He stopped thinking about it.

Luca raised his hand.

The team stopped. The cessation was immediate and total, boots locking mid-stride, weight settling, the passage going so quiet that Eli could hear the pipes. They made a sound. A low, continuous tone, almost too deep to register, like the building's own breathing. He hadn't noticed it until the footsteps stopped.

They were at a junction. Three corridors met here in a space barely wide enough for two people to stand side by side. The overhead light was out. The only illumination came from the passage behind them and whatever leaked from the two corridors ahead, a thin greenish wash that did nothing for depth and made the steel look wet.

Luca stood at the center of the junction with his hand still raised. His head was tilted, not listening exactly, but attending to something in the way the air moved, or didn't move, or carried some frequency Eli could not hear.

Eli pressed his back to the wall. The steel was warm. That surprised him. Everything in this place should have been cold.

The soldier nearest to Luca was Eli's direct subordinate. His name was Hale, Eli recalled. Hale had his back against the opposite wall, close enough to reach Luca in two steps. His hand rested on the hilt of his sword. The blade was half out of the scabbard, an inch of edge showing, the draw a few degrees from finished, and his thumb lay flat against the guard. His eyes moved between the two dark corridors. Then to Luca. Then back to the corridors. Then to Luca again.

Nobody spoke.

Something moved through the left corridor. He heard it before he placed it. A vibration in the grating under his boots, rhythmic, mechanical, growing and then passing and then fading, like a cart on rails somewhere below them, or beside them, or inside the walls. The building doing whatever this building did. The sound peaked and held for three seconds that lasted longer than three seconds, and then it was gone and the junction was quiet again.

Luca lowered his hand. He turned to the left corridor.

It was narrow. The pipes here ran along both walls at waist height, reducing the passage to the width of a single person, and the green light was dimmer, filtering down from somewhere ahead, casting the kind of shadow that made distances unreliable. Luca did not point towards the path they were supposed to take. He simply walked away.

Left.

Hale stood at the opposite wall and did not move. His eyes went to the right corridor. The right corridor was wide, obviously so that Eli could see ten meters into it. The lighting was better, the grating was flat and even, and the walls pulled back enough to give two people room to walk abreast. There were sightlines. Cover potential at the first bend.

Hale looked at it for a long time. His thumb moved across the pommel of his sword in a slow repetitive motion, back and forth across the worn leather.

Hale looked at Eli.

Eli was watching Luca. The left corridor had taken most of him by now. Just his shoulders and the back of his head, growing smaller, unhurried, the sound of his boots on the grating already fading.

Eli did not move for a moment. He too looked to the right.

Then Eli walked after him.

Hale's hand went still on the pommel. His head turned from Eli's disappearing back to the right corridor, to the columns, to the cover, to everything that was there and waiting and correct. He looked at it again and it was the same as the first time. It was still right. It was still the corridor any sane person would choose. His weight rocked toward it, his boot already angling.

He looked back left. Eli was gone.

The other soldier, Wen, put a hand on Hale's shoulder. Hale shook it off.

He went left. His sword knocked against the exposed piping with every step, a thin metallic sound that followed them deeper and deeper into the building.

They walked for another ten minutes. The left corridor fed into a deeper service passage that sloped downward.

Luca stopped at a section where the corridor widened into a shallow alcove, a maintenance bay of some kind, tool mounts on the walls, an access panel hanging open. He stepped into the alcove.

Wen did not step in.

"Stop."

Luca kept walking.

"I said stop."

Luca stopped. He turned, slowly, and the light from the nearest panel caught one side of his face and left the other in the dark of the conduit behind him. He looked at Wen. He waited.

Wen was breathing hard. His hand was on his blade and his back was against the wall and his eyes kept pulling upward, toward the ceiling, toward the floor above them where the boots had been. "This is wrong," he said. His voice was low, pressed thin by the effort of keeping it quiet. "We've been going down for twenty minutes and there is no logic to this route. There are soldiers here. Soldiers with rifles. We are next to them with swords."

Luca said nothing.

"We should have gone right at the junction. We should not be here." Wen went silent. Now the footsteps behind them were so loud it was impossible to ignore. "He's trying to corner us."

Hale, in front of Wen, had gone very still. Eli stood between them and Luca, close enough to both to feel the heat of the conduit and the heat of Wen's breathing and the particular cold that came from the place where Luca was standing.

Luca looked at Wen for a long time. His expression did not change. His hands did not move.

"You hear the patrol," Luca said. Quiet. "They'll come through the corridor to the right and loop back along the service access in four minutes. If that worries you, go. There are only two of them."

Wen stared at him.

"Take care of it."

"There's no cover in that corridor."

"No."

The word fell like something dropped from a height. Wen looked at Luca's face, at the calm of it, at the mouth that had said no the way a person confirms the weather, and his hand tightened on his blade until the tendons stood out along his wrist.

"You're asking me to walk into a corridor with a sword against two armed soldiers with no cover. Does it look like I have a death wish?"

Luca looked at him. Then his gaze moved past Wen, to the overhead panel, to the light it put on the floor, to the place where Wen was standing in the center of it. His eyes rested there.

He reached out and touched the loose bracket on the conduit. It came free without effort. He held it between two fingers, turned it once, and let it fall.

The sound was louder than it had any right to be. It hurt the ears as it rang across the corridor. Eli heard it. Hale heard it. Wen heard it. And somewhere above them, through the grating and the cable runs and the wall panels, someone else heard it too.

Silence.

Eli watched Luca's hand return to his side. Watched it settle there, open, loose. Watched the fingers hang.

The boots started again. Faster.

Wen looked at Luca. Luca was standing against the wall where the conduit formed a ridge thick enough to break a silhouette. Shadow fell across him from the open panel above. He stood in the only dark this corridor offered, and he had been standing there since the beginning.

Wen looked down at himself. He was in the center of the passage. The overhead panel above him was intact and glowing and it put him in a column of white like a performer on stage. He looked at Luca. Luca looked back at him from the shadow of the alcove, and his face was very still, and he did not move to make room.

The patrol came around the bend.

Everything after that happened in the time it takes to draw a breath and not let it go.

Wen was already moving, before Eli saw him choose it, before the lead soldier's weapon had finished coming up, and then it was too fast for Eli to hold and Eli lost him into it.

There was no thought in it. The barrel came up and Wen was already under it, the grating hard beneath his feet, the weapon swinging wide and the flash going off white and silent and then the sound landing all at once, a wall of it that had no edges. Something hot opened along the side of his head. He turned. The second one was closer, the muzzle a small black circle, and nothing in him chose what came next, there was only the push off the floor and the blade going up into the soft dark under the jaw and the give of it, the terrible give, and a wet short sound and the weight dropping off the edge of the steel as the man came down.

Then it let him go.

A weapon hit the grating and skidded. A round had come off the ceiling somewhere in it and passed close enough to move the air through Eli's hair, and he had not had time to move before the fragments came down around him like gravel.

Then breathing. One person. Ragged, from the bottom of the lungs, taking everything with it on the way up.

Wen stood over the two bodies. His sword was wet. His left hand came up to the side of his head where something had taken a piece of skin above his ear, a graze, a quarter inch, and blood was sheeting down his neck and into his collar. His whole body was shaking, a visible tremor that ran from his sword hand through his shoulders and into his jaw, and the blade in his grip knocked against the wall once, twice, and then he leaned against it and slid down until he was sitting on the grating with his knees drawn up and his head between them and the blood from his ear dripping onto the floor.

Then a sound came from back the way they had entered. Low. Final. A single concussion that traveled up through the grating and into the soles of their boots, and somewhere far behind them a door the size of a wall finished closing.

The emergency lights changed. White to a dull red, and the red began to pulse, washing over the alcove and the conduit and the two bodies on the floor, on and off, on and off, slow enough to feel like something breathing.

Eli went to Wen. He crouched beside him and put his hand on the back of Wen's neck and held it there.

Wen was not looking at him. He had lifted his head, and he was looking at Luca, and something came into his face as he understood. "You did this." He pushed himself up the wall to standing, the blade still in his fist, the blood bright at his collar. "You knew it would close. You stood me in that light so it would happen, didn’t you, *Prophet*." He spat the last word.

Luca did not deny it. He let the quiet sit a beat longer than a person should.

"The doors open from one place," he said. "Central control. There is no other." He looked into Wen’s eyes, "Now shall we."

He turned away.

Wen got to his feet. It took him a moment, and Hale put a hand under his arm, and Wen let him. They fell in behind Eli and the team closed up and went on, because the only other choice was for people who had a death wish to wait under that red light.

They walked. The passage fed deeper into the building, the red light pulsing the whole way, and it was a while before Eli heard it.

A scraping. Faint, somewhere ahead, past the next bend. Metal on metal, or something lighter, something with nails. It stopped. Started again. Stopped.

Hale heard it too. His blade was out before Eli could turn, the steel catching the strip light as it cleared the scabbard, and the sound of it drawing was too loud in the corridor. Wen pulled his own blade a half-second later, his left hand leaving the wound on his neck for the first time since the fight, the blood already drying in a dark streak down his collar. They pressed against opposite walls and the corridor between them became a thing with edges.

Luca had stopped walking. He stood ahead of them at the bend, half in shadow, his head tilted. Listening.

The scraping came again. Closer. A pause, then a soft thud, then silence, then something that could have been breathing or could have been a vent cycling or could have been nothing at all.

Nobody moved. The strip light pulsed beneath them. Wen's blade trembled once and steadied. Hale's did not tremble. His knuckles were white and his breathing had gone shallow and controlled and his eyes were fixed on the dark past Luca's shoulder with the particular focus of a man who has already decided what he will do when the next thing comes around that corner.

Five seconds. Ten. The scraping had stopped.

Eli felt the silence the way he felt changes in air pressure, a fullness in the ears, a thinning of the space between moments. His hand hovered near his hip. The threads were there if he needed them. He could feel them coiled at the edges of his fingertips, waiting. He did not call them.

Something moved in the dark past the bend.

Hale adjusted his grip. Wen shifted his weight to the balls of his feet. Eli watched the shadow at the edge of the turn thicken and pull away from the wall and come on toward them, and Hale's blade rose to meet it, and the shape crossed into the red.

A cat.

It came around the corner with its tail up and its eyes catching the red light in two bright points. Grey, short-haired, thin in the way that suggested it ate when it could and didn't when it couldn't. It stopped at Luca's feet. Looked up at him. Continued past without interest, threading between Hale's boots and padding down the corridor behind them as though four armed strangers in a maintenance tunnel were beneath its attention.

Wen let out a breath. His blade lowered an inch. Hale's shoulders dropped a fraction and the air in the corridor shifted toward something that might, in another few seconds, have become relief.

Then footsteps. Fast, light, coming from the same direction as the cat.

"Mochi, I swear, if you went past section nine again I'm going to lose my mind, you know they check the cameras on Tuesdays and if they see you on the feed again they'll..."

A girl came around the bend at a jog. She was mid-sentence and mid-stride and looking at the floor for the cat and she was three steps into the corridor before she looked up.

Hale's sword was level with her throat.

The sound she made was small and high, the sound of a person whose body startles before the voice catches up, and she went backwards so fast she hit the wall behind her with both shoulder blades. The cat, who had turned at the commotion, sat down in the middle of the corridor and began cleaning its paw.

"I'm sorry." Her hands came up, palms out. She was young. Close to Eli's age, maybe younger. Her clothes were layered and mismatched, a jacket too large for her over a shirt that had been mended at the shoulder with thread that didn't match. Her hair was dark and pulled back and coming loose. "I know I'm not supposed to be down here. I'll go. I'm going right now, I just needed to get her before..."

Eli's hand closed around Luca's wrist.

It was fast. He did it before the thought had fully formed, his body turning, his fingers finding the arm at his side and gripping. Luca had been moving forward. Or Eli thought he had. Or Eli's body thought he had, which amounted to the same thing, because the bolt on the grating and the sound it made and the blood on Wen's collar were all still very close, and this girl was pressed against a wall three meters away and Luca was right here.

Luca looked down at Eli's hand on his wrist. He looked up at Eli.

"Lower your weapons, please," Luca said.

He said it to Hale and Wen but he was looking at Eli when he said it, and his voice was even and unhurried and the please landed in the corridor like something from a different building entirely. Eli held on for another second. Then his fingers opened and Luca's wrist slid free and Luca stepped past him toward the girl.

He stopped two paces from her. He did not come closer.

"She doesn't have a uniform," Luca said over his shoulder to Hale, whose blade was still raised. "No identification. No insignia. She's not one of theirs."

Hale did not lower his sword. "She's inside a Citadel facility."

"So are we."

"Nomad, then." Wen's voice was flat. He was looking at the girl's boots, at the mismatched laces, at the jacket patched with the wrong thread. "I haven't seen one in years, because they don't exist. Nothing survives past the barriers." He looked at the girl. "So you're getting help. From Citadel."

"Not anymore," the girl said. Her hands had come down from the air and she was holding them at her sides now, fingers pressed against the fabric of her jacket.

"Not anymore," Wen repeated.

A silence. The cat purred against her ankle.

"They tolerate me down here. As long as I stay below the fourth sub-level and out of the way, they leave me alone. That's the arrangement. That's all it is."

"You need to leave tonight," Luca said. His voice carried the same measured courtesy as before, careful, precise, giving exactly as much warmth as the moment required and no more. "This facility is going to be destroyed. All of it."

Her arms found the cat and gathered it against her chest.

"I can't," she said. "There's nothing outside. Everyone knows it."

"Come with us," Luca said. "We can take you to the other side of the barrier. There's a city there. Beyond the endless fighting. Beyond all of it." He paused, and when he spoke again something in his voice shifted, and it took Eli a moment to place it because it sounded familiar in a way that had no business being familiar. "I'm told there's a garden there that makes people forget they were ever afraid."

Eli stared at the back of Luca's head.

I'm told. He had never seen the Garden. He had never stood in it. And here it was in Luca's mouth, the Garden and the market and the morning light on terracotta, his own words handed back to him by someone who believed in none of it and was offering it to a stranger in a basement as though it were his to give.

Eli stepped forward. He came to stand beside Luca, and the girl's eyes moved to him, and he let them.

"You had a garden," Eli said. "Didn't you."

She went still. Her thumb stopped moving on Mochi's ear.

"How did you know that?"

Eli glanced at her hands. He didn't point anything out. "I have a garden," he said. "It's the most important thing I've ever built. More than the walls. More than the city around it." He held her gaze. "I think when you get there, you will begin to understand."

He stood there, in the corridor, in the red light, with his wings folded and his hands at his sides, and looked at her the way he looked at everyone, which was to say he looked at her as though she were the only person in the corridor and the only person who mattered and the fact that there were three other people and a cat and a building full of armed soldiers was, for this moment, beside the point.

He waited until her breathing had evened.

"No one's asked your name," he said. "I'm sorry for that."

Her eyes moved over the others, over Luca, then came back to him.

"Sable," she said.

"Nice to meet you. I’m Eli," he smiled and held out a hand. "Sable, will you come with us?"

She looked down at Mochi, then up at him. "Mochi has to come with me."

"Of course."

Sable straightened, settling Mochi against her chest, and took a breath and held it and let it go.

"OK," she said. "OK."

They walked.Sable fell in behind Eli with Mochi held against her chest, her footsteps lighter than anyone else's. Hale dropped back to walk beside Wen, and the space they left between themselves and Sable was almost as much as they kept from Luca.

"This is insane," Hale said, low enough that it carried only to Wen. "She could be anyone. She could be planted. A girl and a cat in the basement of a military facility and we're just bringing her along."

Wen said nothing. His hand was back at his ear.

"Metropolis people," Hale said. "You can't trust a single one of them."

Luca glanced back. Just once, just long enough for his eyes to find Hale's. The look was brief. It contained no threat and no warmth and it lasted exactly as long as it needed to for Hale to hear his own words again in the silence between them.

Hale looked away first.

They kept walking. The corridor narrowed and Sable held Mochi tighter and the pipes held their low tone around them and ahead of them Luca walked on, four paces, sometimes five, the same distance he had always kept, as though nothing at all had changed.

Far side of the door, the room was all white and noise.

Hale was good. Eli had said so once and Luca had not disagreed, and now, with the floods burning down off the gantries and the rounds coming in flat across the open floor, Hale made it true. There were screens on three of the walls and they put out a grey light that lied over the consoles and over the floor and over his hands when he set his hands on the console. One screen was dark. In the dark of it he could see the room behind him, small and far down inside the glass, a long grey box with a man standing alone in the middle of it, and he looked at the man for a moment, the small far man, and then he looked away from him too.

His hands found what they wanted on the console. He watched them do it. The doors went shut across the building one after the next, and on the schematic the lines went green to red and the red went to nothing, and it was a small clean motion under his fingers, like turning the pages of something, and when one green line was left he stopped. The last line was the door behind him. He looked at it on the screen and he left it there.

The light above the door went out. He stood in the middle of the grey flashing screens. There would have been something to do now. He stood at the console and did not do it. He did not feel the want of it. His hair came down over his face and shut some of the grey out as the screens went on changing behind their glass, and that was better, and he stayed like that. One breath was the same as the breath before it. There was no way, from where he was, to lay the breaths end to end and measure them, to say whether what went past was a minute or longer than a minute, and the clock in the corner of the near screen counted it and he did not, and his own breathing went on being the loudest thing in the room, and he only wished it would be quieter.

Footsteps came up the corridor. Two, and then one, the near one quick, and they arrived at the door.

He did not move. The door was open still, the last green line, and he let the footsteps come all the way up to it, and for the space of several of them he did nothing and the way in stayed open and anything that wanted to could still come through it. Then he reached out and closed it, and the lock set. It was the only thing he had risen to do since the doors. He put his hand back at his side and was still again.

The first blow came against the steel. Then a voice, then the second one, then both, and the door was thick and the voices came through it the way sound comes through water. Someone was saying his name. Someone was saying Hale's. The blows went on, far off.

"Yes," he said, low, to the ground, to no one. "That's it." A breath. "Almost over."

A voice came through clearer than the others. Wen, he thought, though the steel and the distance made it hard to hold. Sent him in. Shut his communications. The middle of it went somewhere he could not follow. Then, clearer, you will answer for this, and a name, Maeve, and another, Rae, and watching, and something about the council that the door took before it reached him.

He waited until most of it had stopped.

"I have broken nothing." He did not lift his voice to carry it, and was not certain he meant it to carry at all. "We are standing in the control room, and the way out is open.” A pause. “In the morning Auralis will still be there behind its wall. That is the whole of what we came down here for, and it is done."

The blows stopped. A lower sound took their place, close against the steel, a man with his mouth at the seam, so that the words came through whole and quiet.

"You knew, even before you ever said you would come. And if you knew, then none of it was ever going to go any other way."

Luca raised his head, a little, not toward the door, toward the corner he had not looked at since he came in.

"No," he said. "It was not."

"I knew how it ends," His hand lay open on the console where he had left it. "It is the only thing I am ever shown. I am not given the route, or the doors, or which of us will be left standing in which room. Only the end. There is one end, and there has only ever been one."

The door came off its track the first instant the lock started to become loose.

Eli's threads had found the seam and wrenched, and the steel buckled inward and swung and hit the wall behind it hard enough to crack the composite, and the sound of it rang through the control room and came back off the screens and the far wall and died.

Eli stood in the doorway. His wings were out, filling the frame from edge to edge, the tips bent where they met the walls, feathers pressed flat against steel.

Luca was at the console. His back was to the door. The screens were on, grey light on his hands and the floor and the walls. One screen in the bottom corner showed a corridor. A shape on the floor of the corridor that was not moving.

Eli crossed the room and grabbed Luca by the shoulder and turned him around.

"Why."

"His time was up. Delaying would have made things worse."

Eli's grip tightened on his shoulder. He could feel the bone underneath. He could feel how easy it would be to keep tightening.

Wen was in the doorway. The blood from his ear had dried in a dark line down his neck. He was staring at the screen, at the shape on the floor, and his blade was out and his hand was shaking.

"You walked him into that corridor," Wen said. "You walked him in and you came up here and you shut the door."

"The patrol route intersects section twelve on a four-minute cycle. Hale was in the intersection when it came through."

"It happened because you made it happen!" Wen's voice cracked against the walls. "From the first step down you looked at every one of us like we were already in the ground. You forced him into a corridor full of guns the way you pointed me into a corridor full of guns and you watched. You watched both times. What is wrong with you?"

Luca looked at Wen.

"Are you finished," he said.

"Was that the prophecy too?"

I walked into Auralis the same thing I've always been and I will walk out the same thing and everything in between was the path, all of it, including this conversation. Do you feel better? "Don't."

"Don't you stand here and hand me the end of everything like it's already done. You don't get to decide that and call it foresight." His eyes glared like ice. "Look at you. The great savior. "Are you still listening?" Luca’s voice was very quiet.

He closed his eyes.

The dark came first as dark, and then it came as something else. A pressure at his temples, at the bridge of his nose, the familiar weight settling across his eyes like cloth being drawn over a wound. It tightened. It had always tightened. The black wrapped around him and the room disappeared.

The code was there. Emergency destruct. An eighteen-digit string that he had not learned and did not need to learn because it had always been waiting for him, the numbers arriving in sequence as though someone were reading them aloud.

He opened his eyes. Some of the black clung to the edges of his vision, and through it the console swam, the keypad, the screen that prompted the input.

His fingers found the keys. They moved the way they always moved. One digit, then the next. The console accepted each one with a small beep that sounded very far away. Fourteen. Fifteen. Sixteen.

The cursor blinked. The building hummed. The alarm had stopped at some point and he had not noticed. His finger rested on the next key and did not press it.

"Please say something.”

The console accepted the sequence. A new screen. A countdown. He did not read the number. He took his hands off the console and let them hang and stood in the amber light and watched the number get smaller.

The building shifting on its foundations. Then the lights went out. He was in the dark. True dark. The screens were dead. The console was dead. The countdown was still running somewhere but he could not see it.

The ceiling came down in pieces.

The first piece hit his shoulder and drove him sideways into the console. He felt the edge catch him across the ribs. Something cracked. He did not know if it was him or the console. The second piece was larger and it hit the floor beside him and he didn’t feel the need to force himself to keep standing as he crumpled to the floor. He simply lay there. Dust settling on his face. He breathed it in. Then the core went.

It did not sound like an explosion. It sounded like the air being pulled out of a room, a vast inhalation, a pause, and then the push. The shockwave came through the floor and the walls and the ruined ceiling and it picked him up and carried him. He was not aware of leaving the room. He was not aware of the corridor, or the access shaft, or the distance between where he had been standing and the outside. He was aware of the force of it, and the way it held him, and the fact that it was not painful because everything was already painful and there was no room for more.

He hit the ground outside. He knew it was outside because the air was different. Cold. Moving. There was wind on his face and it carried no taste of metal or chemical or recycled nothing. It tasted of dirt and distance and the open sky.

He lay there. Above him the stars were out, or the clouds were out, or something was out, a wash of faint light through the darkness that was starting to cloud his vision. The building behind him was making sounds that buildings should not make, a long sustained collapse, steel folding in on steel, concrete giving way in sections, each one sending a tremor through the ground beneath him that moved through his spine and his ribs.

His breathing was shallow and wet and each inhale caught on something inside his chest that shifted when the air reached it. His hands were at his sides, palms up on the dirt, open the way they had been open on the console. He could feel something slick and warm. His own blood, probably. He didn’t look to see.

The building went on falling for a long time. Then it was quiet.

Luca lay in the dirt outside the ruin and looked at the sky through the black threads across his eyes. His breathing was the loudest thing. It was the only thing. It went on and on, shallow and catching, and the sky went on above it, and the wind moved across his face, and somewhere in the distance a bird was singing, because it was almost morning, and that was what birds did.
